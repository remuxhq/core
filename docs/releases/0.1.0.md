remux 0.1.0, the first release. A CLI and an engine that put a screen, a camera, a
microphone and a music bed into one 1080p30 picture, record it, and send it by RTMP
to Twitch, YouTube or any server, from your own machine, with no account.

## Install

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/remuxhq/core/main/install.sh | sh
```

The script says what it will do and asks before doing it. It needs OBS, because the
engine is libobs: OBS 32 on macOS 15+ (Apple silicon), OBS 30+ from the distribution
on Linux with X11 (Ubuntu 24.04+). It puts `remux` on your PATH and runs the engine
as a service of your session (`remux daemon start|stop|restart|status|log`).
`~/.local/share/remux/current/uninstall.sh` takes it all out, and `--purge` takes
`~/.config/remux` too. Recordings and music are yours and stay.

## What you can do

- **Pick the picture.** `remux sources` lists screens, windows, cameras and
  microphones by the names on them, and `screen`, `window`, `camera` and `mic` choose
  one. A screen is chosen by its display id, never by its place in a list, because the
  capturer and the monitor number the same hardware in different orders. `layout`
  moves the camera (corner, size, square or circle), `card` and `countdown` put a
  "starting soon" card up, and `remux shot --out s.jpg` saves the picture as it is
  right now.
- **Set the sound.** `levels` shows peak meters in the style of OBS (a -60 dB floor,
  green to -20, yellow to -9, red above). `gate` sets the noise gate, `denoise` turns
  on RNNoise, and `screen-sound` sends what the Mac is playing, off by default: a
  music bed is a choice, a call ringing on the screen is not.
- **Play music under the voice.** `music <genre>` plays a folder of yours
  (`~/Music/remux`, one folder per genre), shuffled. The fader is linear in decibels
  and stops at -18 dBFS, because a bed under a voice lives between -60 and -18 and a
  fader that reached full volume wasted its top two thirds. The music ducks 18 dB
  while you speak (`duck` changes how far). `stream-music off` keeps the bed in your
  headphones and out of the live, and a restart never starts the music by itself.
- **Go live.** A destination is a platform and a stream key in
  `~/.config/remux/destinations.json`. The key goes in on stdin (`--key -`), never on
  the command line. `arm` picks where the next live goes, `remux plan` says what
  would happen, `live` starts it and `stop` ends it. Each destination is its own
  ffmpeg, so one that fails or leaves does not touch the others. `sandbox <id> on`
  rehearses on Twitch through its bandwidth-test door: the stream is measured and
  nobody is notified.
- **Record.** `record start|stop` writes one MP4 per session to `~/Movies/remux`
  (`~/Videos/remux` on Linux). The file records what goes out, the card and the music
  included, so it is the record of the live.
- **Stay safe on air.** `panic` takes everything off in one word: a card goes up, the
  screen and the camera go off, the microphone is muted and closed, and the music
  stops. Muted alone is a gain of zero on an open device, and half a guarantee is not
  one.
- **Read the chat.** `remux chat -f` follows Twitch and YouTube in the terminal, the
  platform in its colour and the name in bold when the output is a terminal, plain
  otherwise. A viewer's line is printed as text: escape sequences are stripped whole,
  because an OSC 52 in a message can write your clipboard and a cursor move can
  rewrite a line on a shared screen. `hide <n>` takes a line off every face.
- **After.** `remux history` lists past lives. `remux health` names what stands in
  the way of a live, one line each, including the Screen Recording grant on macOS.
  `remux bug` writes a report an issue needs, with keys redacted, and `--open` fills
  GitHub's form for you to submit.

Every verb takes `--json`, and `remux guide` explains them all, for a person or an
agent.

## Of your own, optional

- `install-relay.sh`: one stream leaves your machine and mediamtx sends it on with
  one `ffmpeg -c copy` per platform. The keys live in `~/.config/remux/byo.env` (0600).
  For two platforms or more on a thin upload.
- `install-chat.sh`: a bridge that reads Twitch (anonymous IRC) and YouTube chat and
  serves it on the engine's chat wire (`docs/wire.md`), for `remux chat` and
  `remux-chat`.

## How the stream is made, and why

- **The timestamps are born at the capture.** Every frame carries the moment it was
  taken, and the sound carries the moment of its first sample. A picture keeps its
  capture moment when that is at least one frame after the last, and otherwise sits
  one frame after it, so no time repeats and no gap is shorter than a frame. Measured
  on a ten-second recording: 298 of 299 frame gaps at 33 ms, where before there were
  34 pairs of frames 0.1 ms apart and holes of up to 90 ms, which a viewer saw as a
  camera that caught.
- **The encoder holds a constant rate.** 6 Mbps, a keyframe every 2 seconds, AAC at
  160 kbps. An average rate let the encoder put 12 to 16 Mbps on the wire on scrolling
  text, and Twitch flagged the live and transcoded it. 6 Mbps is the most Twitch takes
  from a channel that is not a partner, and 2 s is the keyframe interval it requires.
- **The camera is held at 30 frames a second.** A webcam left to its default ran at
  60 into a 30 fps picture: every other frame thrown away, twice the bytes on the USB
  hub, half the exposure per frame, and a picture that trembled and never read sharp.
- **Grants survive an upgrade.** macOS ties a Screen Recording grant to the binary's
  path, so the service starts the engine through the `current` link, which stays the
  same from one version to the next.
- **The GPL boundary is the process.** Linking libobs puts the engine under the GPL,
  and this release is whole: no watermark, nothing locked. Whatever is paid lives on
  servers and reaches the engine through the documented wire, never inside it.
